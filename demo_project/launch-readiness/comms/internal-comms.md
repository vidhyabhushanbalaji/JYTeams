Status: Draft · Owner: Jo Bell · Last updated: 2026-11-13

Who inside Harbour & Heath needs to know what, and when. The launch changes what colleagues see on
their own screens, so the internal comms plan is closer to a training plan than an announcement plan.

## The message in three lines

1. Every customer will see a confirmed delivery date, from stock we actually have.
2. If we cannot confirm a date, the customer is told honestly instead of being given a guess.
3. If something looks wrong, use the escalation path, not the workaround you used to use.

The third line is the one that matters. The old workarounds (typing stock corrections, manually
releasing holds) still exist and would quietly recreate the problem if people keep using them.

## The FAQ

Maintained as a living document with the ten most common questions, updated every Friday during
hypercare. It is kept next to this file in the programme's real documentation system; the answers are
drafted by the person who owns the subject and edited by Jo Bell for tone.

## What we are not saying

| Not saying | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| "This will fix everything" | It will not: store accuracy and carrier capacity remain |
| "The system will be live" | Dates go live; other things stay until later |
| "No disruption expected" | Cutover has a 1 % step for a reason |
| "Please don't contact customers about it" | Customers will ask, and colleagues should answer |

## Measuring whether it worked

| Measure | Target |
| :--- | :--- |
| Contact centre training attendance | 100 % of agents |
| Store task card acknowledgement | ≥ 170 of 184 stores |
| Internal FAQ views | ≥ 1,200 |
| Questions raised to the launch team | ≤ 40, all answered |

The fourth measure is the honest one: if nobody asks a question, the comms did not reach anyone.
